Around this time last year, my life looked completely different.

After almost 19 years of living outside Puerto Rico, I am finally celebrating my first Thanksgiving on the island since I left. What makes it even more special is that, for the first time, I get to celebrate with both my family and my husband's family.

My original plan was to be back in Puerto Rico in time for Thanksgiving last year. However, we received an invitation to a family member's birthday celebration in Colombia and decided to make that trip instead, postponing our return to the island until December.

What followed were some of the most exhausting weeks of our relocation.

Moving from Arizona to Puerto Rico was not as simple as packing a few suitcases and getting on a plane. We had to coordinate with a transportation company to ship our belongings, prepare for our departure, and drive across the country from Arizona to Miami.

We needed to transport our car, but the main reason for driving all the way to Miami was Diamond, our dog. Because of her travel restrictions, flying out of Miami was our viable option.

There were so many moving parts, and everything seemed to be happening at once. As exciting as the idea of returning to Puerto Rico was, the process itself was exhausting. On December 13, 2025, after almost 19 years of living outside the island, I was finally back.

Fast-forward to this Thanksgiving, and instead of coordinating shipments, packing boxes, or figuring out travel logistics, I get to spend the holiday with our families.

Throughout the years I lived outside Puerto Rico, spending time with family often required planning around work schedules, purchasing airline tickets, and deciding how to make the most of a limited number of days on the island.

Now, getting together doesn't have to involve a flight or requesting time off specifically to travel. It's one of those changes that may seem insignificant to someone who has always lived close to family, but it means a lot to me.

I also recognize that being in this position is a privilege. Many Puerto Ricans living abroad would love to spend the holidays with their families but cannot always afford the airfare, take time away from work, or make the financial and professional adjustments that relocating would require.

Having the opportunity to return has changed the way I experience these occasions.

This year, I don't have to count how many days I have left in Puerto Rico before catching a flight back.

I'm simply here.

And for that, among many other things, I am grateful.

Happy Thanksgiving to all the Boricuas celebrating, wherever you may be. 🇵🇷
